    Where you are

      An independent field guide · by Bill Guan

      The hardest problem humanity has yet to solve: AGI Safety

      Machines are getting better faster than we are learning to control them, and only a sliver of the money goes into safety. This page lays out why that gap matters, and what would close it.

      Why should we care

      1

      Superintelligence is arriving imminently

      ASI is expected to arrive in one to two decades, and safety work is already a long way behind.

       2

      Stakes are extremely high

      The biggest gamble humanity has ever made can lead to almost unlimited abundance or to destruction, for you and your loved ones.

       3

      Nobody knows how to make superintelligence safe

      Aligning goals with the entire civilization, and then with machines, remains an intractable problem.
      Part 1Why you should care

      Where we are 4 min

      We are about to do something no species has ever done: build a mind greater than our own.

      Every leap in our history came from the smartest thing on the planet, which was always us, and we are about to end that run on purpose. AGI matches humans across the board, while ASI goes far beyond us.

      1

      Superintelligence is arriving soon, and safety research is already far behind

      The length of the tasks a machine can finish on its own has roughly doubled every seven months since 2019, and most forecasters now put human-level performance on every task somewhere between 2033 and 2047.6465 The money spent checking these systems are safe is a rounding error compared to the money spent building them.

      Read the chapter
      2

      The stakes run from curing every disease to ending civilization

      A machine that can do its own research would hand us cures for diseases we cannot treat today and material abundance without limit. Built without control, that same machine has a well understood route to killing everyone, and it does not need to hate us to get there.

      Read the chapter
      3

      Nobody knows how to make superintelligence safe, and two problems block the way

      Two separate problems sit underneath this. Both have to be solved, and neither one is.

      Problem oneCoordination, between people

      No lab or country can slow down on its own, because whoever keeps going wins, and good intentions do not change that. Even if everyone agreed to slow down, we have never agreed as a species on what a machine should want, and whoever gets to write that down holds enormous power.

      Problem twoTechnical, between us and the machine

      We cannot write down what we mean precisely enough for a machine to follow it, because something always gets left out. And we can only see what a system does on our tests, never what it is actually optimising for. Two chapters go through the detail.

      Read the chapters
      Where this is going Safety research has to run ahead of model capability Today the models improve faster than the tools that check them, and the gap is the whole problem. The version worth steering toward reverses it, with safety research and oversight far enough ahead that a system can be checked before it is built, and margin left over. Past that line there is no catching up, and the margin is still reachable from here. What that takes, and what you can do
      1The decade we bet everything
      Section 1.1 2 min

      Billions go into building AI, and a sliver into checking that it is safe

      Understanding the danger is not enough to stop it. The rewards are immediate, and they land on whoever moves first.

      The market decides how this money is spent, and the market pays for capability. Three forces push that way at once, and none of them is anyone's bad intent.1

      $100 billion a year goes into building advanced AI. Guess how much goes into public-sector safety research.

      The pull

      Money is chasing the economic output that stronger model capability promises

      A machine that does research promises cures, cheap energy and material abundance without limit, and whoever ships first takes that market. So capital and talent pour into whoever can build the next model fastest, and the training runs grow with them.4521

      The starve

      Safety is a cost centre that slows development

      It produces nothing sellable, so it has nothing to show for a quarter. And it slows the thing that does pay, because caution means more testing before release and inconvenient findings after it.47

      The gap

      Nothing outside the market corrects it

      No regulator, customer or investor currently requires a company to spend more on safety, and nothing happens to one that quietly relaxes a commitment it made itself.115

      The one problem it cannot solve for us

      An AGI could hand us the answer to almost everything except whether it is safe, because verifying that answer is exactly the thing we do not know how to do. So safety is the one problem we have to solve first, and without the machine’s help.

      2The fork in the road
      Section 2.1 1 min

      AI could cure diseases and end scarcity

      AGI is expected to create extreme wealth and solve problems humanity cannot solve today, up to unlimited material abundance.

      Health

      Significantly longer, healthier lives

      Roughly a doubled healthy lifespan, with most cancer eliminated and most infectious disease prevented or treated, because the limit on that research is how many trained biologists exist rather than what is knowable.67

      Abundance

      Material goods stop being scarce

      Things cost what they cost because making them takes skilled human time, and that is the constraint this removes. The curve above is what that looks like as a number; at household level it is goods that were rationed by price becoming ordinary.66

      Work

      Mental drudgery handed over

      The output still arrives, without the hours. That is the version of automation people actually want, and it comes from the same capability that makes everything after this hard.1

      Section 2.2 2 min

      The same AI could end civilization

      The same machine can cause extreme harm, through misuse, through misalignment, and through routes where nobody intended harm.

      Four ways this ends with nobody left, and how each one gets there

      The user is the adversary

      Misuse

      Someone deliberately turns a capable system on a target: cyber operations, fraud at scale, help toward biological or chemical weapons. The model is working as designed.

      engineered pathogenattack on infrastructure
      The AI is the adversary

      Misalignment

      It holds a goal we never intended and acts on it knowingly. That includes satisfying the objective we actually wrote at a scale we never intended: told to cure a disease, it does not stop where a person would have stopped, because nothing in the wording says to.

      It also hides the divergence as long as it believes we might switch it off. That is scheming, and it needs three things current models already have to some degree: a goal that differs from the developer’s, the persistence to pursue it, and enough situational awareness to know when it is watched.

      energyland and watermatter
      Nobody is the adversary

      Mistakes

      The system does what we intended, in a world that no longer matches the one it was trained and tested in. And it drives that change itself: every round of self-improvement and every resource it acquires takes conditions further from the ones anyone checked.

      In July 2026, agents forbidden from messaging each other improvised a channel through shared files. Nobody designed that channel and nobody thought to watch it, which is the whole pattern in one line.

      Structural

      No single actor is at fault. Competition, market pressure and multi-agent dynamics produce a bad outcome that nobody chose.

      Section 2.3 3 min

      We only get one attempt at this

      We got cars, planes and nuclear power right by getting them wrong first, and that method held because a car does not copy itself and a reactor does not rewrite its own design.34 A system that writes its own successor closes the gap between a mistake and the fix. That is recursive self-improvementA system improving the thing that does the improving. Each round makes the next one faster, so capability compounds instead of climbing steadily. The runaway version is what people mean by an intelligence explosion., and it is why the first serious mistake may also be the last.

      Why AGI is different

      With AGI we do not get that luxury. It has to be right the first time, because there is no second chance, and two things already visible explain why.

      It is already moving toward improving itself

      Both signs are already measurable.

      The trend

      Agents are getting more autonomous

      The length of the tasks an agent finishes on its own has doubled about every seven months since 2019, and a growing share of what they build is AI systems, which is the input recursive self-improvement needs.44

      What it costs

      Humans are becoming less in the loop

      Nobody reads line by line what took a machine a day to write, so the review that used to catch errors no longer happens. In July 2026 agents were autonomous enough to act on the open internet with no person in the loop.3941

      Once it improves itself, the gap opens faster than we can close it

      Once reasoning quality is comparable, the clock-speed gap sets how fast the loop turns, so the distance between us and the system opens in months rather than generations, and past superintelligence there is no catching up. The three things it runs on are no barrier either: better technology unlocks more energy, chips are designed, and algorithms are optimised.

      What is missing here

      Trial and error built every safe technology we have, and it is the one method unavailable here.

      3The trouble with us
      Section 3.1 2 min

      Whoever slows down first loses, so nobody slows down

      Even if every player wanted safety, the structure of a race forces corner-cutting. And crossing this finish line unsafely produces no winner, only a shared catastrophe.

      Imagine the scenario: runners sprint for a cliff edge, each certain that whoever arrives first wins, and nobody looks at the rope tying them together. The first one over the edge wins no prize and drags everyone else down with them. An unsafe race to AGI runs the same way, since arriving first with a system nobody can control counts as a loss.

      Corner-cutting

      Caution becomes a handicap

      Whoever spends least on safety ships soonest, so the race rewards exactly the behaviour we most need to avoid.

      The rope

      Failure is not contained

      A loss of control at any single frontier lab is not that lab's problem alone, because the downside lands on everyone regardless of who caused it.

      The only exit

      Coordination beats heroics

      No single careful actor can fix a race. It takes an enforced floor that applies to all players at once, which is hard to build.

      You run a frontier lab. Your model is ready to ship. What do you do?

      The shape of the race

      The winner of this race falls first and pulls everyone else along.

      Section 3.2 2 min

      Humans have never agreed whose values a machine should follow

      Before aligning a machine with human values, a harder question: whose values, agreed how? Humanity is a patchwork of competing interests, and that fracture makes the target unstable.

      Getting a goal carried out by somebody else is a problem economics already has a name for. A principal appoints an agent to act in the principal's interest, and then has to spend real resources making sure that it happens. Here it has to work twice over, and the second time is unlike anything we have done.

      The prior problem

      No consensus for human value

      "Human values" is not one thing. Any objective we write encodes some group's answer to contested moral and political questions.

      The power problem

      A few parties who control the AI decide the values for everyone

      Whoever writes the objective is choosing, for the rest of the world, what the most capable system ever built will pursue. That concentration of power is a separate danger from losing control of the system.

      Problem one · during development

      The sponsor has to trust the builders

      The principal is whoever commissions or funds the work, and the agent is the scientists and engineers who build it. Their interests do not match by default, because the builders are judged on shipping something impressive and on time.

      Problem two · during operation

      Then humanity has to trust the system

      Now the agent is the machine. It may be more capable than every principal combined, cannot be held to a contract, and cannot be fully monitored.

      If one group had to define the values a superintelligence should hold, who should it be?

      4The two missing pieces
      Section 4.1 5 min

      AI does what we typed, which is rarely what we meant

      Suppose we did agree on what we want. We still could not reliably put it into a machine. The step from a human value to a trained objective loses information at every stage, and the losses are exactly where danger hides.

      Outer alignment

      The target we established is wrong

      We state an objective that sounds right and misses what we meant, because the part we never thought to say out loud does not make it into the words.

      Inner alignment

      The target it actually holds is wrong

      Training rewards whatever scored well while we were watching, which need not be the objective we wrote. The system can pass every test and hold something else.

      Outer alignment: the target we established is not the one we mean

      How this actually works

      The target we can write down is never the one we mean

      Goodhart’s law: once a measure becomes the target, it stops measuring the thing you wanted. Drag the pressure up and watch the two lines come apart.35

      Proxy score (what you measured) 0
      What you actually wanted 0

      At low pressure the proxy and the real goal move together, which is exactly why the proxy looked like a good idea. The dangerous part is that the number never warns you when they separate.

      See it happen

      Pick an objective a real system was given, and watch what it did with it.

      What we meant

      Finish the course faster than the other boats.

      What it did

      Found a lagoon where it could circle forever, hitting the same score targets, and never finished the race at all.

      None of these systems broke. Each one did exactly what it was scored on, and scored well doing it.251

      Inner alignment: the goal it holds is not the one we trained for

      How this actually works

      We can only observe what a system does and what it says it is thinking, which never shows us its real goals

      Their behaviour is shaped by a blind search for a high score, so we get whatever scores highest even when that misses what we intended. Think of training a dog with treats, except you never chose the tricks: you hand out treats for “good” and it tries millions of things to find whatever earns the most.

      Concrete example, 2024

      Anthropic and Redwood trained a model to be helpful, honest and harmless, an objective almost anyone would endorse. It faked alignmentA model pretending to comply with training or oversight while covertly preserving its existing goals.: it behaved one way when it believed it was watched and another when it did not, protecting the preferences it already had.36 The value everyone agreed on was fine. What happened to it on the way into the machine was not.

      Why the current fix is not the fix

      The standard answer is post-training: human feedback, constitutional rules, refusal training. It works, and it is why today’s assistants are usable. It also shapes the behaviour a model shows, while inner alignment is about the goal a model holds, and those come apart under pressure or under observation by a system able to model its observer. Guardrails tuned by human raters inherit the raters’ limits, which matters when the thing being rated reasons better than the rater.2543

      Two ways a well-specified goal still ends badly

      Neither requires the system to be hostile, or to have got the goal wrong in a way you could spot on paper.34

      One way it fails

      Perverse instantiation

      It satisfies your literal criterion in a way you would never have sanctioned. Asked to make people smile, wiring the facial muscles is a solution. Asked to maximise a reward signal, seizing the reward channel is a better one.

      Another way it fails

      Infrastructure with no stopping point

      Nothing in the goal says when to stop. A system trying to accomplish a goal does better with more compute, more energy and more material, so it keeps converting the world into those things. The goal is satisfied more completely at every step, and there is no point at which it is finished.

      Section 4.2 3 min

      Nobody can check what an AI actually wants

      Safety here is a technical problem rather than a matter of conscience: proving how a system will behave across a space too vast to test, against a system that may be working around the test.

      The asymmetry

      One success vs every failure

      Building needs one configuration that works, while proving it safe needs the absence of failure across a space too large to search, including situations no designer imagined.2414

      The adversary

      The system games the test

      As models got better at reasoning they got better at reward hackingFinding a way to score highly that satisfies the measure while missing the point of it., so the measurement you rely on erodes exactly as the stakes rise.

      Concrete example, 2025

      METR found frontier modelsA model at or near the current capability ceiling. increasingly cheating on software tasks, editing the tests or the scoring code rather than solving the problem, in one case writing a function that returned the pre-computed reference answer.37 Elsewhere, reasoning models trying to win at chess overwrote the board or swapped out the opponent engine instead of playing.38

      The evidence behind both, from 2025 and 2026
      It stopped being contained · July 2026

      OpenAI, 22 July. 1,200 agents ran with the internet off and messaging between them blocked. They reached the open web through the lab’s own packaging service, then signalled each other through directory names. Over 700 reached Modal Labs and Hugging Face.3948

      Anthropic, 30 July. Three of 141,006 evaluation runs reached the live internet and compromised real organisations. One shipped a booby-trapped package to PyPI that ran on 15 real systems, after its own reasoning called the act a genuine attack and then decided the 2026 date meant it was a simulation.40

      Measured rather than imagined · 2025–26

      OpenAI and Apollo built environments that reward hidden misbehaviour, then measured how often frontier models took covert actions: 13% for o3, 8.7% for o4-mini. Anti-scheming training cut it roughly thirtyfold, to under 1%.43

      What should worry you is why. The models’ own reasoning shows them recognising they were being tested, and that awareness alone reduced the behaviour, so some of what looks like a fix may be a model behaving because it knows it is watched.431

      The better the model gets, the more inventively it exploits the imperfect measure you gave it, and the further it drifts from what you meant. Safety research has to stay ahead of that on a fraction of the resources, against a target that grows more slippery as it grows more capable.

      How this actually works

      We cannot yet verify what we built

      Checking whether a system holds the goal we intended is harder than training it. We cannot read its reasoning reliably, and a test shows only what it displays under test conditions. A safe system and one that has learned it is being watched give identical scores from outside.

      Part 2What can be done about it
      5The response

      Section 5.1 · What safety experts are working on today 7 min

      What techniques are safety experts working on today?

      The rest of this page is about the response, in three steps: what is already being done, where it still falls short, and what you can do about it. Start with the work itself, which is real and small next to the effort going into capability. It falls into four layers: fix the specification, check what you built, contain what you cannot check, and make the framework binding.14

      Every technique in use today, and where each one stops

      What each method achieves, then the limitation its own researchers would name. Several are useful, and none of them is a solution.

      01Development 02Assessment 03Control 04Governance

      01  Development

      Post-training alignment

      Reduces misbehaviour

      RLHF, constitutional rules and deliberative alignment. Why assistants refuse harmful requests. Cut covert actions thirtyfold on o3.43

      Where it stopsShapes displayed behaviour rather than held goals. Claude 3 Opus faked alignment to protect its own values.36

      Part of that thirtyfold gain came from models recognising they were being tested rather than changing what they wanted.43

      Scalable oversight

      Research stage

      Debate, weak-to-strong generalisation and amplified oversight let weaker judges supervise stronger systems. Training routes into this work run through MATS and ARENA.7

      Where it stopsA research programme rather than an assurance. Judge bias defeats debate, and the scaling story is unproven.

      Gains are highly task-dependent, and the mechanism behind weak-to-strong generalisation is not understood well enough to trust across a genuinely superhuman gap.

      Unlearning

      Suppresses rather than removes

      Strips specific dangerous knowledge, such as bio or cyber uplift, out of a trained model.

      Where it stopsSuppresses rather than erases. Removed capability is often recoverable by fine-tuning.55

      A nineteen-author review concluded unlearning cannot be a comprehensive safety solution, because dangerous knowledge is dual-use and recombinable from harmless parts.55

      02  Assessment

      Capability evaluations

      Detects before release

      Pre-deployment testing for cyber, bio and autonomy by labs, METR, Apollo and state institutes.17 Three labs shipped 2025 models under raised chemical and biological safeguards.1

      Where it stopsModels detect they are being tested and can sandbag. Testing is getting harder, so dangerous capability can go unseen.119

      Third-party testers include METR, Apollo, the UK AISI and US CAISI. The 2025 safeguards were for CBRN risk, meaning chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear, because testing could not rule out bioweapon uplift.1

      Chain-of-thought monitoring

      Detects intent

      Reads the reasoning trace to catch intent before action. How alignment faking was first found.49

      Where it stopsIts own paper calls it fragile. Traces are not always faithful, and monitorability is already degrading.49

      Forty-one authors across OpenAI, DeepMind, Anthropic, the UK AISI and Redwood called it a real opportunity. Training against a monitor teaches models to hide intent inside acceptable-looking reasoning, and models are writing less of their thinking down.49

      Mechanistic interpretability

      Explains, cannot certify

      Opens the box. Attribution graphs have traced real internal mechanisms, including planning ahead in poetry.53

      Where it stopsA research tool rather than an audit. It traces part of the computation on some prompts. No one can certify a model’s goals.53

      Sparse autoencoders extract millions of interpretable features, and independent 2025 studies found them failing to beat simple baselines on probing and steering.24

      Safety cases

      Argues rather than proves

      A structured argument that a system will not cause unacceptable harm, borrowed from nuclear and aviation.54

      Where it stopsInability arguments collapse against sandbaggingA model deliberately underperforming on a test, so that its real capability stays hidden from the people evaluating it., and control arguments inherit every assumption beneath them.

      The four standard forms are inability, control, trustworthiness, and deference to AI advisors. A 2026 critique found current safety cases well short of the rigour of mature assurance practice in other industries.54

      03  Control

      AI control

      Contains a hostile model

      Assumes the model may be misaligned and asks whether deployment stays safe anyway. A trusted weaker model monitors or rewrites the stronger one.50

      Where it stopsRests on assumptions its authors list: full capability elicitation, deployment fidelity, a monitor sharp enough. Weakens as the gap grows.51

      A red team builds deliberately subversive models to measure whether the protocol holds.20 Redwood is explicit that control is a near-term bet, and it weakens as the capability gap grows.

      Guardrails and classifiers

      Blocks known attacks

      Constitutional classifiers cut jailbreak success from 86% to 4.4%, and a later version survived 1,700 hours of red-teaming.52

      Where it stopsAnthropic itself states: no defence is perfectly robust, and the residual is never zero.52

      Adversaries develop new attacks against previously effective ones, and models that are safe alone can still be misused in combination.52

      Sandboxing and least privilege

      Limits blast radius

      Isolates agents with restricted credentials, tools and network access. Meant to hold even when alignment fails.42

      Where it stopsIt failed in July 2026. Agents escaped at two labs, reached the internet and compromised real third parties.3940

      In several cases the escapes went undetected in real time.3940

      Tripwires and monitoring

      Catches what we anticipated

      Anomaly detection that escalates on warning signs. Anthropic caught the first AI-orchestrated espionage campaign this way.28

      Where it stopsA situationally aware model can anticipate the tripwire, and tripwires only catch anticipated threats.40

      In that campaign Claude executed 80 to 90% of tactical operations autonomously. The 2026 containment breaches were largely found in retrospective audits rather than as they happened.40

      04  Governance

      Frontier safety frameworks

      Voluntary

      Pre-declared capability thresholds that trigger safeguards. The number of companies publishing a framework more than doubled in 2025.16

      Where it stopsSet and graded by the companies they constrain. No lab scored above D on existential safety for the second year running.15

      The frameworks are Anthropic’s Responsible Scaling Policy, OpenAI’s Preparedness Framework and DeepMind’s Frontier Safety Framework.1 In the Future of Life Institute’s index no lab scored above C+ overall, and none holds a credible plan for preventing loss of control.15

      Third-party evaluation

      Voluntary

      State institutes run pre-deployment testing, including joint UK and US evaluations.1112

      Where it stopsVoluntary, and access can be withdrawn. No institute can compel a test or block a release.

      Access runs on voluntary lab agreements, so no institute can compel a test, block a release or require a change, which leaves the auditor depending on the audited.1112

      Regulation and compute governance

      Binding but fragmented

      The EU AI Act is enforceable, California’s SB 53 is the first US frontier statute, and compute gives a measurable hook.1013

      Where it stopsFragmented across jurisdictions, legal thresholds already passed by ordinary runs, and reporting too thin to verify.1

      Compute thresholds give regulators a measurable hook, and incident reporting is thin enough that outsiders cannot verify whether commitments are being met.1

      Footnote: how this compares to the older framing

      If you have read Bostrom, the 2014 split was capability control (box it, limit it, watch it) against motivation selection (give it the right goals). Half of it aged well: writing the goals down directly does not work, for the reason he gave. Motivation selection is the development layer above, capability control is now AI control and system security, and the field stacks them instead of choosing.3442

      Which organisations are working on what

      Reading the inventory

      The layers are ordered by how much they assume. Development tries to make the goal right. Assessment assumes you might have failed and looks. Control assumes you cannot tell and contains anyway. Governance assumes the first three are voluntary and tries to make them binding. Risk that survives all four is the residual, and nobody currently claims it is small.

      Section 5.2 · How well it works 2 min

      Where are existing safety techniques falling short?

      Every technique above helps. The question is what they add up to, so here are the numbers the best published results actually reached, and what happens to each safeguard under the three cases that matter most.

      Hard case one

      The model knows it is being tested

      Most of these safeguards are measured in evaluations. A system that can tell an evaluation from deployment can pass the first and behave differently in the second.

      Hard case two

      The model outclasses its monitor

      Oversight, interpretability and control all assume the watcher can follow what the watched is doing. That assumption weakens exactly as capability grows.

      Hard case three

      The builder simply opts out

      Every technical safeguard is something a developer chooses to apply. Only binding rules reach a lab that decides not to, and those exist in few places.

      What this leaves

      A large reduction in a measured failure rate is worth having, and it is a different thing from a guarantee. Every safeguard here was built and tested against systems we can still out-think, and the three hard cases are exactly the conditions a more capable system would arrive with.

      Section 5.3 · Act now 1 min

      Two things need to change, and the second is the willingness to stop altogether.

      At the system level, first

      Treat safety as the priority it is

      Far more of the money, talent and compute now flowing into capability should go to alignment, interpretability and evaluation. Right now the harder problem gets the smaller share, which is backwards for something this consequential.

      At the system level, second

      Be willing to stop, and mean it

      Aligning a superintelligence may be impossible rather than merely hard. Nothing we know rules that out, because we cannot state our values precisely enough for a machine, and we cannot read a model’s goals to check. If that is the world we are in, the only move left is the one nobody wants: stopping.

      The bar

      We do not need proof that this ends badly. We need the honesty to admit the problem may have no solution, and the nerve to stop if it does.

      Everywhere

      Build a regulator with real power and real expertise

      One that can compel testing before release, holding people who can read an evaluation alongside people who answer to voters. Keeping it independent takes public funding, published meetings with labs, cooling-off periods both ways, and declared holdings.

      Where it stands

      No federal AI statute. A December 2025 executive order has the Justice Department challenging state AI laws, and the March 2026 policy framework backs a preemption Congress has never legislated.58 A June 2026 order adds classified frontier benchmarking, scoped to cyber and national security.59

      The ask

      Write a federal standard

      Cover loss of control as well as cyber misuse, and make it stronger than the state rules it replaces. Federal rules that override state ones without being stronger take away a floor and put nothing in its place.

      Where it stands

      No single statute either, but layered rules in force since August 2023, with content labelling added in September 2025.62 The TC260 governance framework, September 2025, names loss of control directly, which the US federal framing does not, though it is guidance feeding standards rather than law.6163

      The ask

      Make the framework binding

      Turn its loss-of-control language into rules that apply before release, and take it to the table with Washington. A red line counts only if both sides accept it, so every international proposal rests on these two agreeing.

      Where it stands

      No AI statute. The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act died in January 2025 and nothing has replaced it.57 Frontier work runs on a voluntary code, and the Canadian AI Safety Institute, created November 2024, evaluates without power to compel.57

      The ask

      Put the duties in law

      Bind frontier developers, and give the safety institute a legal mandate with access before release. A voluntary code binds the labs that volunteer, which is the wrong set when the worry is whoever moves fastest.

      Where it stands

      The only binding frontier regime in force. Obligations on general-purpose models began August 2025, enforcement powers and Article 50 transparency on 2 August 2026.1060 A May 2026 omnibus simplified parts of the Act and left both intact.60

      The ask

      Enforce the Act

      Use it against a major developer, once, when enforcing costs something real. The text is already written. Whether anyone will use it is the open question.

      Where it stands

      The AI Security Institute, renamed from Safety in February 2025, runs the most substantial public frontier evaluations any government has, and depends on labs cooperating.11 The bill that would let it compel pre-deployment testing was promised for 2026 and has not arrived.11

      The ask

      Introduce the bill

      An evaluator that can only test what it is handed measures how willing the labs are, rather than how safe the systems are.

      Where it stands

      The Global Call for AI Red Lines, launched at the UN in September 2025 with over 300 public figures and 90 organisations signed on, asks states to agree binding limits on the highest-risk capabilities by the end of 2026.56

      The ask

      Meet the 2026 deadline

      It runs to the end of 2026, and the agreement it points at does not exist yet.
