Why this exists

How to read this

Two people, five arguments, no referee

The main argument on this site takes a position and defends it. This page does the opposite job. Each of these is a live disagreement written the way it was actually argued, interruptions and concessions included, and the reading offered at the end of each is exactly that, a reading rather than a ruling.

One thing worth being clear about

These are not opposite answers to the same question

Sam’s view and the view on the rest of this site are often taken as rival verdicts on whether AI is dangerous. They are mostly not. One is an argument about how fast capability can physically arrive and which harm lands first, and the other is an argument about whether the control problem gets solved at all. A reader can hold both at once, and the interesting disagreements are narrower and sharper than the usual framing suggests.

What would change this pageIf capability growth continues at pace through the grid constraints described in Conversation 01, the bottleneck argument weakens considerably. If it stalls for a decade, the case on the main page gains time it currently does not assume it has.
